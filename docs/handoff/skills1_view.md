## 3. The ring (design v1 2.3 and 2.4)

**What a cat shows.** Every cat whose `UnitDef.spSkill` is set (only built skills) wears a ring round its class sticker. `SpRings.tick` reads `UnitState.sp`, `blocked` and the battle clock; nothing else.

* **One frame sheet**, one texture: frames 0..23 are the bare track and the arcs (1/24 of a turn per frame, clockwise from 12 o'clock, a cream rim, a dark track, an orange arc), frame 24 is the closed ring (gold with darker edges, a white hairline just inside), then the flash (a white ring) and the halo (a flat mustard disc in two steps). `ringFrame(gauge)`: 0 for a gauge of 0 or less (or NaN), `floor(gauge * 24)` up to 23, 24 when the gauge is 1. A ring is a sprite that swaps its texture when the frame changes; nothing is drawn per frame.
* **Three moments.** Filling: dark track, orange arc, nothing else. Full and waiting: the closed ring, a flat halo, and for the first four of them a swell of 7% and a halo that breathes. Just cast: a white ring flashes over the sticker (0.4 battle seconds, grows to 1.12x), the gauge is already 0 (the simulation said so), and the cat shrugs (0.36 s: 1 to 1.12 to 1 in size, 4 px up).
* **Not colour alone.** An arc against a closed ring, a white hairline, a halo, a swell, a pause mark on a stopped cat, a star mark under reduced motion, and the gunner's holstered chip.
* **Place.** Centre `RING_AT = (-30, -27)` from the cat's feet, radius to the line's middle 15.2, line 4, rim 0.9, so 18.1 outside. **The class sticker of a cat with a ring moves there** (from `CLASS_AT = (-38, -19)`): the ring's line covers the sticker's cream border. This was forced by the rank tag: every skill cat is epic or better, and a legendary or mythic tag's rounded end runs under the old place; the new one clears all five tag lengths by at least 1 px at rest and 0 at the top of the pulse and the flash (a test holds this, with `ringClearance`). A cat without a skill, or any cat in a battle without skills, keeps its sticker at `CLASS_AT`.
* **Inside the cell** (108 x 96) at rest, at the top of the pulse (1.07x) and at the biggest of the flash (1.12x), and inside the cell's paper (98 x 86) at rest; clear of the buff badge column (x 22 to 48) and of the special-cell badge. At 450 x 900 a design px is 0.625 px: the ring is 22.6 px across and its line 2.5 px; at 360 x 800 it is 18.1 px and 2 px.
* **Follows the cat.** The ring is a child of the cat's own view, so a drag, a hop, a merge, a molt, an awakening or a sale carries it; it takes the sticker's pop-in (scale and opacity) so it arrives with it; a view that is given another cat is attached again (`attachRing`): a cat without a skill hides the ring and puts the sticker back, a skill cat gets the same ring back, reset.
* **Pulse limit.** `PulseRoster`: at most `PULSE_MAX = 4` full rings pulse, **the first four to become full in the order they became full** (ties: cell order); a ring that casts or whose cat leaves gives its place to the next one waiting; a stopped cat's ring does not count. A ring that starts or stops pulsing eases its swell in and out. The pulse clock (`PulseClock`) follows the battle clock but never runs faster than 1.5x real time, so the swell stays under 3 a second at 3x; a popup stills it. (Seen on the real screen: five full rings on the test board, `spState().pulsing` lists four.)
* **Stopped.** `UnitState.blocked`: the ring is tinted grey and wears the `stopped` mark (two bars).
* **Holstered (review pass).** A gunner whose gauge is full while the gunners hold their fire is full but not ready. `SpRings.begin` keeps it out of the pulse roster (it takes no one's place), and the ring shows it three ways: its own teal closed ring (frame 27 of the sheet, `HOLSTER_FRAME`, cream rim and white hairline like the gold one), no halo and no swell, and a bigger mark (the pistol chip is 28 design px, 14 px on a 360 px screen; the two bars and the star stay 18). It goes back to the gold pulsing ring the frame the target comes out.
* **Pulse phases (review pass).** The pulsing rings do not swell in lockstep: ring number `n` of the pulsing ones (in the order they became full, `PulseRoster.slot`) is `n x PULSE_PERIOD / PULSE_MAX` ahead of the first, so four full rings make a ripple, not a wall.
* **Marks.** `RING_MARK_RULES` is keyed by skill id and asked once a frame per cat with that skill (keep it allocation-free). A stopped cat's mark wins over the rule's, the rule's over the reduced-motion star. Marks are 18 design px (11 px on a 450 px screen, 9 px on a 360 px one), except the holstered pistol (28 design px). Today: `stopped`, `ready` (reduced motion), `holstered` (the gunner, 6.5).
* **Reduced motion** (`motion.reduced`): no swell, no halo breathing, no shrug, a flash that fades in place, and a full ring wears the `ready` mark (a still star). **Low tier** (`fxSettings.tier === 'low'`): no halo, no swell, a flash that only blinks.
* **Cost.** Per frame `SpRings.begin` walks the 25 cells once (the roster) and each ring compares a few numbers; a ring that is not pulsing or flashing touches no property. One `RingLook` object is refilled for every cat (nothing allocated per frame). The sheet is 1176 x 672 px (392 x 224 design px at 3x), painted once; with skills off it is never painted.

## 4. The cast director

`mountSp(stage, on)` (called by `createDirector`, returns null and registers nothing when `!battle.spOn`) listens to `spCast`, `spRunHit`, `spRunBurst`, `spRunEnd`, `bite`, `victory`, `defeat`.

### 4.1 The stage rule, the dispatch, the clean-up

1. **The stage rule** (`StageRule`): at most one cast effect per 0.25 s of **real** time (`stage.now`). A cast that loses shows no scene, no sound, no shake: only the ring flash, which the field draws for every cast whatever the stage says. A lost cast takes no slot (the next gap is counted from the last SHOWN one). **The first cast of a skill in a run is exempt**: it is always shown with its scene, its name bubble and (rank 5) its cut-in, and it takes the slot like any shown cast. (Otherwise a bubble could appear with no picture.) At 3x with six vikings full in the same tick (a real battle): 6 casts, 3 shown, 3 lost; the unit test drives 72 casts a real second through the rule and gets about 8 shown in 2 s (4 a second, as at 1x).
2. **Dispatch.** The scene of `e.skill` (the registry in `scenes.ts`) gets `cast(e)`. A run's follow-up events (`spRunHit`, `spRunBurst`, `spRunEnd`) go to the scene only if the run's cast was shown (`runId` is remembered, forgotten at `spRunEnd` and on clear); `bite` goes to the `wanted` scene if the last gunner shot was shown (the gunner's scene also listens to `bite` itself, 6.5). Every scene gets `update(dt, battleDt)` once a frame.
3. **Clean-up.** `victory` calls `clear()` on every scene and the intro (a scene may be used again after a revive); **`defeat` clears every scene and the intro except a scene with a run in flight** (`SpScene.survivesDefeat`: the tiger's and the star archer's): the simulation freezes a run at a defeat and carries it on after a `revive`, and every picture is timed by the battle's clock, which stands still, so the body, stomps, stars and the night freeze with it and go on (the run ids are kept so the events after the revive find their scene). The director's destroy calls `destroy()` and removes `window.__dbg.spStage`. The field's rings clear their listener and flashes when the field is destroyed.
4. **The view fails alone (review pass).** The simulation emits `spCast` after the gauge is spent and before the damage, and an emitter does not catch, so every call into a scene or the intro is guarded in `index.ts` (`guarded`): the error goes to `console.error` (which `window.__errors` records) and a scene that threw is cleared and switched off for the rest of the battle (`stats().broken`). A skill with no scene takes no slot of the stage rule.

### 4.2 Time (read this before drawing anything)

Every picture is timed by the **battle's clock**: `BattleApi.time` (the scenes get `battleDt = time - lastTime`), which stands still under a popup and a hit-stop and runs 2x / 3x with the game. A scene that ages a thing uses `Age` and `spanK(battleAge, realAge, battleLife, realFloor)`: **a picture lasts until both its battle seconds and its real floor are over**. At 1x the battle seconds are the longer; at 3x a 0.4 s picture would be 0.13 s, so the floor (about half the life) keeps it readable; under a popup the battle age stands still and so does the picture. The ring's flash and shrug use the battle clock alone (`momentK`), and the pulse clock is capped (section 3). Particles and floating numbers are the fx pool's: they run on real time (existing behaviour), are short, and obey their budget and priorities. Tweens (the cut-in, banners) run on the real clock.

### 4.3 The name bubble and the cut-in (`intro.ts`)

* **The bubble** (`SpIntro.bubble`): the skill's name on a paper label in the class colour with a tail, over the cat's head, once per skill per run (`e.first`), for every skill below rank 5 whether or not it has a scene (a rank 5 skill shows the cut-in instead; the bubble stands in only for a banner that was dropped). About 0.8 s: 0.8 battle seconds with a floor of **0.6** real seconds. It pops, rises 10 px, fades; under reduced motion it only fades. At most four live at once (the oldest goes). **It stays on the board** (`bubbleSpot`): sideways the whole label is kept inside the board (the tail keeps pointing at the cat), and a cat in the first row gets the label on its own body instead of on the road above, where the cast acts. It is in the numbers layer, over the cast's own pictures.
* **The cut-in** (rank 5 only: `unitDef(id).spSkill.rank === 5`, `introFor`): the awakening's own (`fx.awakening(portrait, name, { short: true, tag: t('rarity.mythic'), color: class colour, nameSize, passThrough: true, tempo })`), 0.8 s at 1x, once per skill per run, with the existing `awaken` sound. **It takes no taps** (`passThrough`: a press that starts under it reaches the board; the awakening's own banner follows the player's own action, a skill's arrives uninvited) and plays 1.25x as fast at 2x and 1.5x as fast at 3x (`cutInTempo`). **It never cuts another banner short and is never dropped for one**: rank 5 casts are spaced 1.2 battle seconds apart, which is 0.4 real seconds at 3x, so a skill whose banner finds one playing waits its turn in a queue (`SpIntro.waiting`: at most `CUTIN_QUEUE` = 3 wait, none longer than `CUTIN_WAIT` = 3 real seconds; a banner that cannot wait leaves the bubble in its place). The queue is emptied by `clear()`.
* **Long names (landing pass).** The banner's name label is about 380 px wide and cuts what it cannot shrink to 0.7 with "...": the tiger's English name came out as "The Tiger Has Ar...". `cutInNameSize(name)` measures the name once at 58 and passes `nameSize` (`nameSizeFor(width)` in `fx/cutin.ts`): a short name keeps 58 (a cat's own awakening never sets the option), a long one starts smaller so that the label's own shrink fits it ("The Tiger Has Arrived!" now reads whole, in a smaller type).

## 5. How a scene or a ring mark is added (step 2: eight more skills)

**A scene.**
1. Write `src/view/director/sp/<name>.ts` that exports `make<Name>: SpSceneFactory<'<skill>'>` (`(env: SpEnv) => SpScene<K>`). Copy the shape of `shove.ts` (a class with `cast(e)`, the optional `runHit`, `runBurst`, `runEnd`, `bite`, `update(dt, battleDt)`, and `clear()` / `destroy()`); a running body copies `charge.ts` / `lap.ts` with `runTrack.ts` and `runPieces.ts`; a hold on the boss bar copies `wanted.ts` with `biteHold.ts`. `SpEnv` is `{ stage, ctx, battle }`: `stage.fx.ps.burst(def, x, y, mods)` and `stage.fx.shockwave(...)` for particles (a module-level `EmitDef` per recipe, priority 0 or 1), `stage.play(stage.rules.big, id, ...)` for a sound (`heavyHit` where `big` would drop it at 3x), `stage.shake(Trauma.t1, Gate.spShake, 0.3)` for the camera (not under `stage.reduced`), `stage.detail` (0 low, 1 mid, 2 high) to thin things out, `stage.reduced` for reduced motion, `ctx.layers.fxBack` (under the enemies and cats) and `ctx.layers.fxFront` (over them) for pooled sprites and graphics, `battle.enemies` / `battle.units` for the live state.
2. In `scenes.ts`: import it and put it in `SCENES` under the skill's id. Nothing else changes: the dispatcher already does the stage rule, the intro, the run bookkeeping, `update` and `clear`.
3. Rules of the house: pool what you draw (make on first use, reuse, a cap), age things with `Age` / `spanK`, hide everything in `clear()`, destroy what you made in `destroy()`, never move an enemy or a unit (the simulation does), draw from the event's numbers and the public state only; the stage rule has already decided, so `cast` is called only for a shown cast.
4. Test it like `tests/view.director.sp.test.ts` does for the shove: stand-ins for the stage and the layers, `vi.mock('@/fx')` (with `original()` spread, so `NAME_SIZE` and friends stay) for `fxTex`, a stub `document` whose 2D canvas does nothing if the scene paints a texture.
5. Facts the sim hand-off gives (its section 6): tiger leap `x, y` -> `landX, landY`, place stomps and paw prints from `spRunHit.x/y` not from `head`; arrow: a star per `spRunHit`, never from the arrow's place; gunner: poster at `data.x/y`, hold the boss bar at `enemy.hp + bite.amount`. The body's place at battle time `t` is `pathPoint(startPath + dir * min(dist, speed * max(0, t - at - delay)))`.

**A ring mark** (the gunner's "holstered" is the example): add the id to `RingMarkId` (`spRingMath.ts`), its picture to `MARK_DRAW` (`spRing.ts`: a function `(ctx, cx, cy)` that paints a 24 px sticker on a 2D canvas; the table is typed `Record<RingMarkId, ...>`, so the compiler asks), and its rule to `RING_MARK_RULES` keyed by the skill id; add the id to the marks test.

## 6. The four scenes

Times are battle seconds from the cast; at 3x everything runs a third as fast, with no picture shorter than its real floor (4.2).

### 6.1 The viking's shove (design v2 3.1, `director/sp/shove.ts`)

* **The shield slams.** A round wooden shield with a steel rim, drawn in code (`shieldTexture()`, painted on first use; the painted `badge_shield` is the cobalt blue of an ENEMY's shield, which no friendly effect may wear) lands in front of the cat toward the road (big and quick, then holds and fades), with a ring in the warrior colour and dust at the cat's feet. 0.34 battle s, floor 0.2 s.
* **A wedge of dust over the road.** `roadInReach(x, y, range)` scans the loop for the stretches whose centre line is within `data.range` of the tile (the event carries no road interval), and `runsAround` cuts each down to the part the pushed enemies stand on (read from `EnemyState.travelled`) with 44 px more at each end: the reach of a corner cat is one long stretch round the corner, and the wave is drawn only where something is pushed (a white line down an empty road read as a glitch). One pooled `Graphics`: a flat kraft wedge from the cat to the road, a lighter inner wedge, and a cream band along the road (the wave's front), drawn once and then only grown from the cat (0.2 to 1) and faded: 0.42 s, floor 0.24 s. Along each stretch 3 / 5 / 7 puffs of dust (by tier), the farther ones later (900 px/s), as if the dust ran out from the cat.
* **The pushed enemies.** The enemies really slide in the simulation; the scene adds a **ghost** (the enemy's own picture at 62% opacity, fading) at `pushed[i].x/y`, a **swipe** on the ground from there to `toX/toY` (the fx atlas's `streak`, as long as the slide), and a few speed lines kicked toward where it stood. At most 4 / 8 / 12 enemies by tier, the first in the cast's order. **An elite or a boss braces**: a ring and dust at its feet, a fainter ghost, no speed lines; its slide is shorter by the simulation's own factors.
* Sound `hit_heavy` (and a quiet `whoosh` 0.06 s later) through the stage's `big` rule, a T1 camera nudge through `Gate.spShake`. Pieces: 3 wedges, 2 shields, at most 24 slide pieces, reused.

### 6.2 The two running bodies (tiger and star archer)

* **Where the body is (the one formula).** `pathPoint(startPath + dir * min(dist, speed * max(0, t - at - delay)))` with `t` = `BattleApi.time` and every number from the cast's payload (`SpRunData`, `SpChargeData`, `SpLapData`); it is `runPlace(track, t, out)`. `pathPoint` wraps, so the seam of the road and the four corners are no special case. Because `t` is the battle's own clock the picture is exactly where the simulation's body is at 1x, 2x and 3x, holds still under a popup and goes on after it. The tiger leaps along a straight line from the tile's feet to `landX / landY` by `(t - at) / leap`; the run starts at `at + delay` = the leap's end. Stomps, stars and bursts come from the events with their own `x, y`: a star or a stomp is never drawn from the body's place (the tiger's first tick also touches enemies 63 px behind the head, and the arrow passes enemies that get no star).
* **Two runs at once** (a tiger and an arrow overlap: rank 5 casts are spaced 1.2 s and a run is 1.2 to 1.4 s): each has its own body (up to three of each kind; a fourth would cut the oldest), and the night stays until the last arrow is done. Runs are told apart by `runId`.
* **A run cut off by victory or defeat** sends no end event: the dispatcher's `clear()` hides every body, stomp, print, star, burst and mark, gives the cat on the tile its full opacity back, and lets the night lift by REAL time (the battle's clock does not move again). A scene is usable again after a revive.
* **Drawn inside the screen (review pass).** The tiger's 1.5x body is drawn pulled in from the left and right edges the way every big body of the field is (`bodyX` on the body's drawn width; its logical place, `Runner.x / y`, is still the road's and what the tests hold it to); the stomps are placed at the enemy's drawn place (`bodyX`), like the shove's ghosts and the poster.
* **Gentle and switchable.** The night is `NIGHT_ALPHA` = 0.35 (it was 0.42; below the flash guide's cap of 0.45 so that the rings and the cats keep their colour; a dusk, not a night); the tiger's shake is one T1 nudge per cast; under reduced motion there is no night and no shake, the tiger's leap is flat with no squash, the outline does not breathe, and the arrow, its ribbon and the stars still run. **Low tier**: no gait prints or dust for the tiger (the stomps stay, 8 at most), a short ribbon and no glints for the arrow, 2 victim stars, 18 stars at most (mid: 14 stomps / 32 stars, high: 22 / 44; bursts 3 / 5 / 8).

The poster keeps its own tick. No "-6%" for a bite that killed.

A poster slaps on 1.85x on an ordinary target and 1.5x on a boss (`SLAP_BOSS`), so a boss's body is not hidden while the cork flies.

The dispatcher's routing is unchanged.

**The "holstered" mark.** `RING_MARK_RULES.wanted = holsterMark`: a gunner whose gauge is full while the gunners hold their fire. `holdingFire(battle)` is the simulation's own rule worked out from public facts (an ordinary wave whose next wave brings an elite or a boss and is not the last wave of a run with an end; in an elite or boss wave, until the target has come out), worked out once per wave and battle. ### 8.3 Cost

How it was measured (headless Edge, **software-rendered**, so absolute numbers are not phone numbers; the on-against-off difference is what is worth reading). Results (the first three of each four runs, which still have enemies on the road; the fourth run of each was quiet because the board had cleared the road and is left out): **typical load** (gauges as they fill: 12 to 13 casts shown in 48 battle seconds) skills off 4.1 ms per frame (runs 3.3 to 4.7), skills on 4.8 ms (4.3 to 6.1): **about +0.6 ms, +15%** of a frame that carries three simulation ticks; **stress load** (every gauge refilled every second: 30 shown, 71 to 77 lost by the stage rule per 48 battle seconds) off 4.2 ms, on 4.2 ms: no difference the measurement can see (it is good to about 0.5 ms).
