## A4. The landing pass

### A4.1 Scenes are built on their skill's first cast

The step-1 review (12.2 C6) asked for it once there were twelve scenes. `mountSp` used to make every scene at battle start (twelve layers, pools and listeners on a board that mostly never casts half of them); it now makes none, and `sceneFor(id)` builds a skill's scene on the first `spCast` of that skill **whether or not the stage rule then shows that cast**. Why this is safe: the first cast of a skill in a simulation is `e.first`, which the stage rule never drops, and a director belongs to exactly one simulation, so the cast that builds a scene is shown to it; a scene that listens to `spCast` itself (the ninja's, to seat the clones of a cast that lost) has not subscribed yet when that very event is delivered (the emitter iterates a copy) and does not need to. Follow-up events (`spRunHit`, `bite`, a scene's own `cutOpen`, `cloneThrow`...) only ever follow a cast. The scene's layers are added to `fxBack` / `fxFront` at that moment, so their order among scenes is the order of first casts instead of the registry's: nothing depends on it (the night and the dusk insert themselves at index 0, posters and the cloud find the night by its label and sit above it, and every scene's own pictures are ordered inside its own container). A factory that throws is logged through `console.error` (which `window.__errors` records), the skill gets no picture for the rest of the battle and takes no slot of the stage rule, and the factory is not asked again. The dispatcher's tests (`tests/view.director.sp.test.ts`) now check: nothing built at mount, one scene per first cast and only that one, a lost cast still builds its scene and shows nothing, a throwing factory, frames and clean-up only for the scenes made; `tests/view.director.sp.lazy.test.ts` does the same with **the real registry** (twelve entries, none built at mount: no scene, no listener beyond the dispatcher's seven, not one node on any layer; a `cut` cast builds exactly the samurai's scene with its one listener) and checks the ring holds are let go when the director goes; the six mount rigs read a scene's `live()` through `tests/lazyScene.ts` when asked, not at mount. On the real screen `stats().made` goes 0 -> 8 in the first tick of a board with every gauge full (one scene per skill that cast) and reaches 12 as the rest cast.

### A4.3 Found on the real screen and fixed

1. **A skill's cut-in banner made every HUD button dead while it played.** Seen with a real tap: the pause button needed 4 taps in the middle of the twelve-skill board (the first three arrived under banners), and a probe showed 11 taps in a row over about 4 s all dead while the tiger's banner was on and the first tap working the moment it was gone; with skills off, a `passThrough` banner started from the console behaved the same (6 dead taps). Cause: Pixi's hit test lets any visible display object under the press swallow it whatever its own event mode (the banner's `dim` is the size of the screen), and step 1 had only set the banner's `hit` sprite to `eventMode: 'none'`; the field's own input reads DOM pointer events, so the board kept working and the bug was invisible there. Fix (`fx/cutin.ts`, one line): with `passThrough` the whole banner layer is `eventMode: 'none'`, which prunes it from the hit test; the banner that a player's own awakening plays (no option) still takes the tap and skips. Test: `tests/fx.cutinPass.test.ts`. Seen after: one tap pauses with the tiger's banner on screen (`banner-tap-before.png`, `sheet-pause-tap-ko-450.png`).
2. **"One summon's worth, ring at half" was cut with an ellipsis** (the lucky cat's fish capsule, English, at every width: the label's cap is in design pixels). All 36 capsule lines in both languages were measured with the real font and `PaperLabel` (`lines.mjs`): only this one exceeded the label (394 against 380). It now reads "A summon's worth, ring at half" (368). `npm run font` was run (unchanged).
3. **The ring-hold store was never cleared.** `ringHold.ts` is module state keyed by a unit's `uid`, the next battle numbers its units from 1 again, and nothing called `clearRingHolds()` (it said "a battle is over"). Both scenes that hold a ring let go in `clear()` and `destroy()`, so a leak needs a scene to fail mid-cast, but a stale hold would show a stranger's gauge on a cat of the next battle: the dispatcher now clears it when it goes (test in `view.director.sp.lazy.test.ts`).

## A5. How to stage a scene (the debug helpers across the scenes)

Route `?debug=1&scene=battle&chapter=1&seed=7&lang=ko&sp=1&runs=5&sandbox=1` (`sandbox=1` skips the laser pointer's coachmark). Facts that cost time: `__dbg.battle.step` does not tick the prep, and on a profile that has played before the prep countdown is not done after a stepped 2.4 s (hold, step 2.4, then let it run in real time until `phase` is `wave`); a boss or elite arrival freezes the battle for its banner (real-time tweens: wait until `step(0.1)` advances the time); the first-encounter card must be dismissed (`quiet(b)` in `tools/edge/lib.mjs`); a `step` runs the scene's update and not the tweens, so a banner (real time) must be waited for in real time; a dynamic `import()` of a module after a hot update is a second instance of it (read a module's state through the page's own objects). `put(cell, id, gauge)`, `gauge(cell, 1)`, `gaugeAll(1)`, `crowd(n, id, from, gap, hp)`, `elite(id, from, hp)`, `boss(id, from, strip)`, `chill(frozen, slowed, seconds, from)`, `capsule(kind, inner)` / `capsule()` (real odds back), `relicAll()`, `hold`, `step`, `setSpeed`, `spState()`, and `window.__dbg.spStage` (`stats()` = `{ shown, lost, runs, broken, made }`, `scenes.<id>.live()` once the scene is built, `intro.pending()`). To make a stage-end toy screen close in a long run, tap its first card (the API's `pickRelic` leaves the screen open and the clock held).

### 4. The gap the picture has to cover (read this before drawing a skill whose damage the simulation does at the cast)

The simulation does the knock in the tick of the `spCast`: the damage is dealt, the `hit`s are sent and the chill is spent before the glass has left the paw. If the scene drew only the glass, the player would see the "-15" and the health bars drop and the dead enemies' puffs half a second before the shard. So:
1. **Ice held on every shard's enemy** (`Ice`): the enemy's own tint and sticker are gone in the cast tick, so the scene puts back what the chill looked like (a block or a frost coat, and the snowflake or clock sticker at the place the enemy view draws it) until the shard lands. The ice follows its enemy by uid.
2. **The numbers wait** (`numberHold.ts`, the combat director's `say`): the scene HOLDS an enemy's numbers when it makes the ice and RELEASES them when its shard lands; the combat director asks `defer` for every number it is about to show. What it holds is added up per style and colour, so a held enemy shows ONE number. A hold nobody released (a cleared scene, a bug) ends by itself after 1.5 battle seconds and shows what it held; `clear()` (victory, defeat, a broken scene) shows everything at once. A battle clock that is not a number never ends a hold.
3. **An enemy the cast kills** (the common case: an ordinary enemy against 3 to 5 D) is killed by the simulation in that same tick, and its ordinary death (the director's puff, the fish flying to the counter, the field's shrinking body) is not the scene's to hold back. So the frame after the cast, when the enemy's `hp` is 0, its ice takes the enemy's own picture (tinted pale) as a **frozen statue** that stands until the shard shatters it; the number comes with the shard. What the player sees: the puff and the fish come first, the statue in its ice shatters 0.4 to 0.7 s later. It reads as "frozen, then smashed" much better than the empty ice it first showed (seen: `k9` against `k10`).
4. The shards that do not fit the tier's limit (8, 14, 22) have no ice and hold nothing (their numbers show at once); the frozen ones keep their ice first. ### J2. The bard, "다 같이 합주!" / "Jam Session!" (`jam.ts`)

### L4. Choices

1. **The partner capsule's colour is MINT GREEN** (the data row says `'mint'`; it was `'sky'`). The design asked the lead to choose a colour apart from the ninja's clones (pale sky blue), the toy's gold and the pinks. Mint stays 90 degrees or more from each of them (a test holds the distance against `GHOST_SKY`, the gold and the pink). `SpCapsuleRow.marks[].color` and `tests/sim.spGround.test.ts` changed from `'sky'` to `'mint'`: a label only the view reads, no rule or number. The owner can see it on the candidate sheet.
2. **The paper lines are two lines**, the cry and a smaller line under it, because the design's one-line drafts (up to 28 syllables) would have shrunk to 14 px on a phone. Every draft keeps its words; `partnerFull` was split at its comma. The English "A legend becomes a guardian" is **"A King becomes a Guardian"** (the game's own rank names, decision 28). Numbers are `{n}`, filled from the event's `purr`, so the data row stays the owner of them. The English fish line is "One summon's worth, ring at half" (the draft's "ring back to half" did not fit the label).
3. **A cast the stage rule dropped shows nothing of the scene** (not a quiet opening): the same rule as the other scenes. The prize pictures that belong to other systems still play.
4. **A defeat does not tear a capsule down** (`survivesDefeat`): the simulation keeps the capsules in the air for a revive; the picture, timed by the battle's clock, freezes with them. A victory drops them.
5. **The partner falls from the sky in the field, not in the scene**: `tossFor('skill')` is `SKILL_DROP_SECONDS` (0.32), and `Arrivals.drop` flies the sticker, so a twin whose cast the stage rule dropped still falls. Reduced motion has no flight (as for every toss).
6. **The ring is held, not redrawn**: `ringHold.ts` is a tiny store (`holdRing`, `releaseRing`, `ringHeld`) that `SpRings.tick` asks (`ringHeld(uid) ?? u.sp`). The bard's scene uses it too. If a jam note lands on a lucky cat while its fish capsule is in the air, the note's release shows the real gauge for a moment before the lid; seen nowhere, noted.
7. **The toy screen waits 0.4 s for the lid** and the lid runs on the real clock for that long. The drag the player may be holding is let go by the field's own check (`blocked()` already includes a pending choice); a drag on a cat that a promotion or a free awakening replaces is let go by one new line in `input.ts` (the cat under the finger is no longer the one on the cell).
8. **No coin rain** (the design: the lucky cat's passive draws that), no new sound, no camera nudge (a capsule is not heavy).
9. **What the chorus does not do:** the closed-eyes face and the little body wobble of the design's purr chorus are not drawn (a hearts-and-rings chorus instead): a face on top of 25 different cats is art, not code.

### L8. Debug calls that stage it

`window.__dbg.battle.capsule(kind, inner?)` fixes the kind of the next capsules (`'fish' | 'purr' | 'partner' | 'more' | 'toy' | 'guardian'`; for `'more'` the second argument is the small capsule's kind, default `'partner'`) and `capsule()` with no argument puts the real odds back. Stage the rest through the board and the gauge: `board({12: 't_lucky', ...})`, `gauge(12, 1)`, `crowd(...)`. The prizes that come out as fallbacks are real ones: a partner with no cat to twin (a board of lucky cats only), a guardian with no King to awaken (the same), a toy with every toy owned (`relicAll()`); a **full board** makes the partner promote a cat where it stands; a board with warriors of three kinds and two samurai (`{6: 'w_sword', 7: 'w_viking', 8: 'w_samurai', 11: 'w_samurai'}`) gives the guardian someone to awaken. `window.__dbg.spStage.scenes.capsule.live()` returns plain data: `flights` (kind, small, state, stage, x, y, scale, rot), `beams`, `stamps`, `glyphs`, `swells`, `pops`, `springs`, `wash`. A sim step before the prep has ended (about 2.5 s) does nothing: step until `phase` is `'wave'`.
